Harvest Ledger Trust
The brief

Turning cash into specific food, not vague goodwill.

  • Give by the item. Donors should see food items priced at market rate, pick what and how much, then pay that exact total.
  • Prices move. The NGO needs to revise per-item pricing themselves, without touching code.
  • Campaigns steer demand. The food bank wants to push a particular grain or seasonal drive from the same portal.
  • Reach beyond the website. A printable QR code for malls and partner storefronts, and room to onboard partner organizations.
03 / 11
Harvest Ledger Trust
Our solution

One portal, two vantage points: a shelf to give from, a ledger to run it from.

  • For donors. An item-level catalogue (wheat, rice, pulses) with quantity controls, so a gift is tracked as food, not as a rupee figure.
  • For the food bank. An admin dashboard to add or retire items, revise prices as the market moves, and read back donation insights.
  • For campaigns. Banners the admin controls directly, pointing donors at whatever is running short.

Why item-level

Naming the grain instead of an amount turns a donation into a decision: the same instinct behind a gift registry. It also hands the NGO a live signal of what's stocked versus what's needed.

Access model
Who does what

Two roles, one shared ledger.

Donor

  • Adds any quantity of any food item to the cart
  • Checks out by paying the exact donation amount
  • Sees campaign banners and links across the portal
GIVES TO

Administrator

  • Views and updates the overall inventory
  • Marks an item as bought or fulfilled
  • Adds, edits or retires items and their pricing
  • Authenticates via JSON Web Tokens
