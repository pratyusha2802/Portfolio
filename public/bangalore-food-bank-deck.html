The Problem

Giving food shouldn't require guesswork.

Bangalore Food Bank needed a way to turn goodwill into groceries — letting donors choose specific items at current market prices, instead of handing over an undirected sum and hoping it stretches far enough.

What donors needed

A place to browse listed food items — each priced at current market rate — select what to give, and pay only for the items they chose.

What the NGO needed

Control to update prices as the market shifts, run campaigns that spotlight specific items, and see donations arrive already sorted by inventory.

Reach, physically

A printable QR code the NGO could post across malls and partner storefronts, dropping people straight onto the donation portal.

Reach, institutionally

Room to bring other organizations on board, so the same portal could carry more than one partner's donation drive.

Our Solution

One portal, two clear jobs.

Donors see an inventory they can actually trust. Administrators get a control room for that inventory. Neither has to guess what the other is doing.

01

Track real quantities

Every donation is logged against a specific item — wheat, rice, dal — in the exact quantity the donor chose, not a lump sum.

02

Give admins control

A separate dashboard lets the NGO add or retire items, reprice them as markets move, and read back donation insights.

03

Steer attention with banners

When one item runs low, the admin can put it front and center — no engineering ticket required.

Roles

Two people, two very different screens.

A donor's whole job is choosing and paying. An administrator's whole job is keeping what's on offer accurate — and JWT authentication keeps that second job locked down.

Donor

Gives with intent

  1. Adds specific food items to a cart
  2. Sets the quantity of each item themselves
  3. Checks out by paying the donation total
  4. Sees interactive banners and campaign links across the portal
←
Administrator

Keeps it accurate

  1. Views and updates the overall inventory
  2. Marks items as bought once fulfilled
  3. Adds, edits, or retires food items
  4. Signs in through JWT-based authentication

Salient Features

What actually ships.

 

1

Item-level giving

Bridges the gap between donors and the NGO by letting a donation target a specific food item and quantity, not a vague sum.

2

Full admin control

Administrators log in to manage item counts and quantities and keep the whole inventory organized in one place.

3

Campaign banners

A dedicated admin feature for spotlighting a particular item — useful the moment one category runs short.

4

Auto-calculated totals

The cart adds up exactly what's owed as items go in, so donors always know their total before checkout.

5

Secure checkout

Payments run through Razorpay, so donors can pay with confidence and the NGO gets a verified record.

Future Scope

Where this goes next.

Three additions that would turn a hackathon build into an ongoing operations tool.

Volunteer accounts

Registration and login for volunteers, so field help isn't limited to donors and admins.

Live market pricing

An external API keeps item prices current automatically, instead of manual admin updates.

Inventory visualized

Charts and dashboards over the raw inventory data, so trends are visible at a glance.
